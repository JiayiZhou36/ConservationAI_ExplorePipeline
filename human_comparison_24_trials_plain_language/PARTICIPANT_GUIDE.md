# Participant instructions

## What is this study about?

We are studying different ways to remove or hide animals in photographs.
Researchers use computer software to find animals in photographs. To test how
reliably this software works when a picture changes, we remove or cover an
animal and check how the software responds.

In this study, we want to learn how realistic those edited pictures look to
people. Your task is to judge the photographs. You do not need to know anything
about the software or image editing. There is no expected answer.

## What will I see?

Each page shows three images:

- **Original photograph:** before editing. Use it to see where the animal or
  animals were. It is only a guide and cannot be chosen as an answer.
- **Edited Image A:** one version made to remove or hide the animal or animals.
- **Edited Image B:** another edited version of the same photograph.

Some edits fill in the area where the animal was. Others cover the animal with
a black patch or make that area blurry. The animal may still be partly visible.
A and B can change positions on every page, so look at both each time.

## How do I answer?

1. Look at the original to find the animal or animals before editing.
2. Compare A and B. Look at the edited area and how it fits the whole scene.
   You can select any image to open a larger view in another tab.
3. Answer **“Which edited image looks more like a real photograph?”** Judge how
   real it looks, not just how well the animal is hidden. Consider anything
   unusual, such as edges, colors, shapes, or blurry patches. Choose A, B,
   **“Both look equally real,”** or **“Cannot judge.”**
4. Answer **“Which edited image do you prefer overall?”** This is separate from
   how real it looks. Your answer can be different. Choose A, B,
   **“No preference,”** or **“Cannot judge.”**
5. You may leave a comment about what looked real or unreal. Do not include
   personal information such as your name or email.
6. Select **“Save answers and continue.”** Answers are saved before the next
   comparison appears. Submitted answers cannot be changed.

Wait for all three images to load. If an image does not load, refresh the page.
Take the time you need. There are no right or wrong answers.

## How many comparisons are there?

There are 24 comparisons, with eight for each of three animal species. Each
comparison has two questions. The page shows your progress. When you finish,
you will see a message confirming that your answers were saved.

## Can I stop or come back later?

Taking part is your choice. You can stop by closing the page. Answers already
submitted remain saved. Your browser can remember an unfinished session for
up to 30 days using a cookie. Return using the same browser. Clearing cookies
or using another browser may start a new session. Please take part once.

## What information is saved?

The app saves a random code for your browser session, which images you saw,
their A/B positions, your answers, optional comments, and timing. It does not
ask for your name or email. The hosting service may separately keep website
access logs.
